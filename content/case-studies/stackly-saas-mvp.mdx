## The client

Stackly helps small agencies keep track of client assets: logins, licences, domains and renewals. Its founder, Sara, had run an agency herself and knew the problem well, but had never built software.

## The challenge

Sara had a clear idea, a fixed budget and an investor demo booked in ten weeks. She needed a real product that teams could sign up to and pay for, not a clickable prototype, and she needed to understand every trade-off along the way.

## What we built

<FeatureHighlight
  title="Built for teams from day one"
  image="/images/projects/stackly-saas-mvp/dashboard.svg"
  alt="Stackly team dashboard with projects and recent activity"
>
  Every account is a team, with invites, roles and a shared dashboard. Data is separated per team at the database level,
  so growth to hundreds of customers needed no rework.
</FeatureHighlight>

- A one-week design sprint that cut the feature list to what the demo and first customers actually needed.
- Sign-up, team invites and roles, with magic-link login.
- Stripe subscriptions with a free trial, upgrades and invoices handled automatically.
- An admin dashboard for Sara to see sign-ups, usage and revenue at a glance.

## Technology choices

Next.js, TypeScript and PostgreSQL gave us one codebase for the product and the marketing site, hosted on Vercel with previews for every change. We wrote a short decision log in plain English, so Sara could explain every choice to investors.

<Callout>The demo ran on the live product, not slides. Stackly closed its pre-seed round a month later.</Callout>

## The results

Stackly launched in nine weeks and had 120 paying teams by month three. Because the foundations were right, there was no rework after launch; every sprint since has gone on new features.
